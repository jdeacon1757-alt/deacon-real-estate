How buying new construction works

  • Pick a community and lot: the lot can change the price as much as the floor plan, so compare premiums, views and what's being built nearby.
  • Choose a plan and options: structural options are usually decided first, then design selections. Upgrades add up fast, so set a budget before the design appointment.
  • Sign the builder's contract: builder contracts are written by the builder, not the standard Indiana purchase agreement. Read deposit, timeline and price-change terms closely.
  • Inspect during the build: consider a pre-drywall inspection and a final inspection before closing, even on a brand-new home.
  • Close and use the warranty: keep track of the builder's warranty and walk-through lists after you move in.

Questions to ask every builder

  • What's included in the base price, and what is an upgrade?
  • What are the lot premiums, and what will be built behind and beside this lot?
  • How much is the deposit, and when is it refundable?
  • What's the estimated completion date, and what happens if it's late?
  • Do you offer incentives for using your preferred lender, and what are they?
  • What does the warranty cover, and for how long?

Why bring your own agent

The builder's sales team works for the builder. Your own agent works for you: comparing communities, reviewing the contract, pushing on incentives and keeping the build on schedule. FAQ

Common questions

Which home builders are building in Westfield, Indiana?

Builders with homes in Westfield include Estridge, Lennar, David Weekley, Drees, M/I, Fischer, Beazer, Olthof, Pulte and Silverthorne. Which communities each is selling in changes often.

Do I need an inspection on a new construction home?

It's a good idea. Many buyers get a pre-drywall inspection during the build and a final inspection before closing.

Should I use the builder's lender?

Builders often offer incentives for using their preferred lender. Compare that offer against an outside lender's rate and fees before you decide.

Can I bring my own agent to a builder?

Yes, and it's recommended. Ask the builder about its agent policy before your first visit, since some require your agent to be with you from the start.
