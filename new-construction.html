The builder has someone in their corner. Do you?

When you walk into a model home, the friendly person at the desk works for the builder. Their job is to sell homes at the best price and terms for the builder. The contract you'll sign was written by the builder's attorneys, to protect the builder.

That's not a knock on builders; it's just how it works. But it means one side of the deal has a professional looking out for them, and unless you bring your own agent, the other side doesn't.

Your own agent levels that out. In most new construction purchases, the builder pays the buyer's agent commission. Ask the builder to confirm in writing how agent commission is handled before you sign.

Bring your agent from the very first visit

This is the step most buyers miss. Many builders require your agent to be with you, or registered, at your first visit to a community. If you walk in alone and sign the guest card, you may not be able to add an agent later.

What I do for you on a new build

  • Compare communities and builders: base prices, what's included, HOA dues, lot premiums and what resale homes nearby are actually selling for.
  • Pick the right lot: the lot can change your price as much as the floor plan. I'll look at what's planned behind and beside it and how it affects resale.
  • Read the builder's contract with you: deposits, timelines, price-change clauses and what happens if the build runs late.
  • Negotiate: incentives, closing costs, upgrades and lot premiums are often where there's room to work.
  • Keep you from overspending on upgrades: set a budget before the design appointment and focus on the upgrades that are hard to add later.
  • Stay on the build through closing: I'll help you set up inspections during construction and walk the final punch list with you.

How buying new construction works

  • Pick a community and lot: compare premiums, views and what's being built nearby.
  • Choose a plan and options: structural options usually come first, then design selections.
  • Sign the builder's contract: it's the builder's own contract, not the standard Indiana purchase agreement.
  • Inspect during the build: consider a pre-drywall inspection and a final inspection, even on a brand-new home.
  • Close and use the warranty: keep track of the builder's warranty and walk-through list after you move in.

Questions to ask every builder

  • What's included in the base price, and what is an upgrade?
  • What are the lot premiums, and what will be built behind and beside this lot?
  • How much is the deposit, and when is it refundable?
  • What's the estimated completion date, and what happens if it's late?
  • What incentives do you offer for using your preferred lender, and what's the rate and fee compared to an outside lender?
  • What does the warranty cover, and for how long?

FAQ

Common questions

Do I need my own agent to buy new construction?

It's strongly recommended. The builder's sales team works for the builder and the contract is written by the builder. Your own agent works for you: comparing communities, reviewing the contract, negotiating and staying on the build through closing.

Does it cost more to use my own agent on a new build?

In most new construction purchases the builder pays the buyer's agent commission. Ask the builder to confirm in writing how agent commission is handled before you sign.

When should I bring my agent to the builder?

At your first visit. Many builders require your agent to be with you, or registered, the first time you visit a community, and may not let you add an agent later.

Do I need an inspection on a new construction home?

It's a good idea. Many buyers get a pre-drywall inspection during the build and a final inspection before closing.
